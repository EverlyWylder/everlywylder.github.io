Silver Hollow

A visitor's leaflet. Spoilers have been removed by Guest Resolution.

Welcome to Silver Hollow, Colorado: a silver boom town up a side canyon off the interstate, about an hour west of Denver, with a Victorian main street called Front Street, a creek through the middle, and mountains close enough on both sides that the sun leaves early. Prospectors came for gold in 1859 and found pyrite. The silver strike of 1864 made the town. "Boom Town" is the park's marketing, not the town's history, and the locals will tell you so.

Boom Town

A small family-owned 1880s gold-rush theme park built on and over the old mine. Ride the mine train through real tunnels, get soaked on the sluice flume, catch the saloon revue, lose yourself in the mirror maze in the old assay office, and meet Buckley the Beaver on Main Street. The fudge shop is better than it has any right to be. Open Memorial Day to Labor Day. If something goes wrong, Guest Resolution is behind the sheriff's office facade. Ask for Agnes. Not the lady with the cookies. The other one.

The Ambrose

The grand old hotel, built over the hot springs in 1908 by a patent-medicine millionaire who promised miracles from the "radium water". The miracles stopped in 1932. The hotel kept going: nightly ghost tours, a bar with a long story for every photograph, a wedding most weekends, and a hot-springs terrace where you soak while the bighorn sheep stand on the rocks above and watch you. The sheep have a webcam. The sheep have a gift shop.

Silver Hollow Rails

A narrow-gauge steam train that starts inside the park, goes through the mine tunnels, comes out above town, loops down Front Street, stops at the Ambrose, and comes back. It runs on a timetable, which in this town is the same thing as an alibi.

The Silver Hollow Mine Museum

One room and a half in the old powder house behind the carousel. The 1899 lantern is in its case there, with the 1950s Geiger counter from the uranium rush and the roll of the eleven men lost in the collapse that sealed the lower level. The Geiger counter goes off now and then for no reason anyone can give. The staff have stopped mentioning it.

The Boom Town Carnival

When the park closes for the season, the crew doesn't. The Boom Town Carnival takes the rides, the showdown and the fudge wagon on the road: a haunt in October, a Christmas carnival in California, a winter festival and a renaissance faire in Arizona, a folk festival in New Mexico, and home in May to open the gates again. Every stop is a new town and a new set of suspects.
